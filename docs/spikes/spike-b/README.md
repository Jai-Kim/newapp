construction, not by convention.

Not vague callbacks; chapter 2 picked up precise facts it could only have read:

The engraved-words payoff is the strongest evidence: chapter 1 recorded only that
the words *hinted at* a prior owner. Chapter 2 invented who that was and why —
and did so consistently with every other stored fact. One thread was formally
resolved with `resolved_chapter = 2`, verified in the database rather than taken
from model output.

## Korean — native, not translationese

This is the part that could most easily have failed, and didn't.

- **Mimetic vocabulary throughout** — 톡톡 (rain tapping), 조르르 조르르 (oil
  pouring), 송송 (raindrops beaded on feathers), 축 처졌어요 (shoulders drooping),
  스르르 (a feeling melting away), 방긋 (a small smile). Sound and manner words are
  the signature of real Korean children's writing, and the first thing missing
  from machine translation.
- **Consistent 요-form narration**, the right register for reading aloud to a
  5–6 year old.
- **Native phrasing over literal equivalence.** "The oil has to climb the wick,
  thread by thread" becomes "기름이 심지를 **한 올 한 올** 타고 올라가야 하거든" —
  한 올 한 올 is the idiomatic strand-by-strand phrasing, not a calque. Grandma's
  "Some things take exactly as long as they take" becomes "어떤 일은 **딱 걸릴 만큼
  걸린단다**", which lands the same aphorism the way a Korean grandmother would
  actually say it, including the -단다 elder-to-child ending.
- **`누비` (Nubi)** — a natural Korean name that also evokes 누비다, to quilt or to
  roam; apt for a round, padded owl that wanders to the window.

Page alignment holds: every page's EN and KO narrate the same events.

## Emotional shading drifted between languages — found, and fixed

The first run (archived in [`before-beat-fix/`](before-beat-fix/)) produced page 2 as:

- **EN** — "It's **not fair**. I did everything."
- **KO** — "**이상해.** 나는 다 해 봤는데." ("That's **strange**. I tried everything, though.")

Same events, different feeling: injustice versus puzzlement. That satisfied the
ADR's "same events per page", but not the product's actual job — a parent picks
tonight's lesson to land a specific feeling, so two language tracks teaching
slightly different emotions are two different books, and the drift compounds
across a chapter and across months of chapters bound into a keepsake.

Treated as a bug. Fixed by constraining the *beat* rather than weakening native
composition — one sentence added to the system prompt and to
`docs/prompts/story-generation.md`:

> Both language versions of a page must land the same EMOTIONAL BEAT as well as
> the same events … Express that beat in whatever phrasing is most natural to
> each language; never translate the phrasing. The feeling is the lesson, so the
> two versions must not teach a different one.

Same page, after:

- **EN** — "…and her voice went small and **cross**."
- **KO** — "목소리가 작고 **뾰로통**해졌어요."

`뾰로통하다` is precisely sulky/cross — a native match, not a translation of
"cross". And the beat is now tracked *across* pages: page 7 closes the arc with
EN "found she wasn't cross anymore" / KO "**뾰로통한 마음이 스르르 풀렸어요**" ("the
sulky feeling melted away"), reusing the same Korean word to complete the same
emotional shape. No cost or latency change.

## Wardrobe split

Wardrobe varies per page while identity stays in `children.character_ref` —
and as of Spike A's `04-wardrobe-swimsuit.png` this is proven at the **image**
layer too, not just in the text. Explicit identity-vs-wardrobe prompting produces
the swimsuit, bare feet and boots-set-aside that plain instruction could not.
ADR-0001 §5's premise holds.

comparison — the ADR's "text roughly doubles, negligible next to images" holds.
Illustrations parallelise; the text call does not.

## Not done

- **pgvector k-NN retrieval.** Retrieval is recent-N + all open threads. With one
  prior chapter that is sufficient and proves the mechanism, but it does not
  exercise semantic recall — the interesting case is chapter 40 recalling
  chapter 3. Flagged as a TODO in `generate-chapter`.
- **Embeddings are never written.** `chapters.embedding` stays null; nothing
  computes it yet. Required before k-NN retrieval means anything.
- **No safety pass.** Spike D's content filter and parent-preview gate are still
  a TODO in the function.
- **Korean assessed by me, not a native speaker.** My read is that it is
  genuinely natural — 한 올 한 올, 뾰로통, and the -단다 ending are the kind of
  detail translation misses — but the pass bar says "reads naturally to a native
  speaker", and that deserves a human check. Jai's family is the right panel.

## Reproducing

```bash
cd docs/spikes/spike-b
python3 run_ch1.py    # opens the lantern threads
python3 run_ch2.py    # separate process; continues them from DB canon only
```

`CHILD_ID` in `spike_b.py` points at the seeded test child (Mia, 5–6, `ko`).
Re-running appends chapters 3, 4, … rather than replacing — clear the child's
rows in `chapters`, `threads`, `characters`, `world` and `lessons_taught` first
for a clean run.
